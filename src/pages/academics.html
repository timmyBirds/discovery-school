Programmes

One school, from crèche to graduation.

Nursery & preschool

Crèche · M1 · M2

Little ones explore numbers, letters and God's world through thematic units — butterflies, Lake Tanganyika, and more. Each topic is taught in English and Kirundi one week, then French and Kirundi the next, so the youngest learners hear all three languages in familiar contexts.

Primary

P1 – P6

English is the principal language of instruction each morning for most subjects. In P1–P2 children learn to read in their mother tongue first; Kirundi continues through primary. Afternoons include French several times a week, with Swahili introduced during some of those sessions.

Secondary

Middle school G7–G9 · High school G10–G13

Rigorous academics, research skills, technology and a growing load of cross-curricular projects. Students sit the IOWA test at the end of middle school and the SAT at high-school graduation. A programme brochure is available from the office.

English immersion & trilingual learning

Fluent in English. Rooted in Kirundi. Confident in French.

English immersion from primary is one of the things families come to Discovery for — and one of the reasons our graduates can study anywhere in the world.

But immersion does not mean losing your own language. Children learn to read first in their mother tongue, study Kirundi throughout primary, and keep Burundian history, geography and government at the heart of the curriculum. French is studied throughout, and Swahili — the language of the region — is introduced in primary.

The result is a graduate who can communicate with confidence in the languages of Burundi, of East Africa, and of the wider world.

English

Language of instruction for most subjects from primary. Learn, research, lead and connect internationally.

Kirundi

First reading language in P1–P2, then studied throughout primary. Value and engage Burundian identity.

French

Taught from preschool and throughout the school. Burundi's official language of administration and higher education.

Swahili

Introduced during primary afternoon sessions — the trade language of East Africa.

Teaching for every learner

See it, hear it, touch it, do it.

Children do not all learn the same way, so our teachers plan lessons in multiple modalities — so that auditory, visual, tactile and kinesthetic learners all have a way in.

Auditory

Discussion, story time, songs, recitation and read-alouds.

Visual

Diagrams, charts, models, films and virtual field trips.

Tactile

Manipulatives, laboratory work, sewing, art and building projects.

Kinesthetic

Drama, sport, field trips around the CEEM compound and beyond.

Students design t-shirts, build model homes, make films, do carpentry, and run experiments in the science laboratory — hands-on learning is not an add-on but the way we teach. See student life

Library

An inviting space to study, or to relax and read a fun book. The library holds more than 9,000 books, and we hope to add more over time. Students from Grade 3 upward may borrow books to take home; younger children enjoy weekly story time, and teachers check out books to read aloud to their classes. Tablets are available in the library for research.

Technology skills

Two computer labs — one mainly for primary, one for secondary. Computer class begins once a week in P1, twice a week from G7, and daily in Grade 11 for IT credit. After Grade 10 students apply their skills in cross-curricular projects, and one hour a week is set aside for research with the lab available.

Learning support

Extra help when a child needs it.

Special in-school lessons are available for students in P1 to G9 who are struggling in reading or mathematics, or who have a learning disability.

Students are supported inside the school day by trained staff, in coordination with their class teachers and parents. Ask the office or your child's teacher about support lessons.

Test preparation

Resources to help secondary students prepare for the IOWA and SAT are collected on the family resources page, including an SAT preparation webinar, a Digital SAT overview, and IOWA practice questions.

Outcomes

Ready for tomorrow.

Discovery graduates are trilingual critical thinkers with a Biblical worldview, prepared to take part in national, regional and global communities.

International testing

IOWA at the end of middle school and the SAT at graduation give every student — and every university — an internationally comparable measure.

Diplôme d'État equivalence

Graduates who achieve the required standard on their final international exams receive equivalence to the Burundian government diploma.

University-ready

Alumni have gone on to universities in Burundi and across Africa, Europe, Asia and North America.
